# Curriculum goal
At this progress point, players enter a multi-floor alien ruin containing evaporation and condensation machines. On the first two floors, players become familiar with how the two types of machines operate and how adjusting their settings affects the water system within the puzzle.

To solve each puzzle, players must determine an appropriate combination of evaporation and condensation settings so that the system reaches the condition needed for the key cube to enter the lock station and unlock the next room or floor.

On the third floor, players are expected to apply what they learned from the earlier practice floors with greater independence. Their performance is evaluated partly by how many interactions or adjustment attempts they require before successfully configuring the evaporation and condensation machines. Fewer unnecessary adjustments indicate a stronger understanding of how the two processes interact.

The fourth floor introduces a more complex configuration involving evaporation and condensation, requiring players to apply the same scientific concepts in a more challenging system. Performance on this floor is also included in the assessment for this progress point.

The curriculum goal is to strengthen players’ understanding of evaporation and condensation as complementary phase-change processes within the water cycle. Players learn that evaporation changes liquid water into water vapor, while condensation changes water vapor back into liquid water. By adjusting the two processes and observing their effects on the system, players practice reasoning about how changes in evaporation and condensation influence the movement and distribution of water.

The increasing complexity from the earlier floors to Floors 3 and 4 is intended to move players from guided familiarization toward more independent application of these concepts.

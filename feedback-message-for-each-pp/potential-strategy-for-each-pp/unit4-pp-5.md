# Information and Strategies Available to Players

1. After leaving the alien ruins, players learn that Anderson needs help because her bunker is flooding. Players first shut off the fountain that had been pumping groundwater, but the bunker continues to fill with water. This observation provides an important clue that the fountain is not the only cause of the flooding.

2. Players can use the in-game chat-history tool to review previous conversations containing information about the fountain, groundwater, the bunker, and surrounding subsurface conditions. These conversations can help them reconsider possible explanations for why the flooding continues.

3. While exploring the flooded bunker, players collect evidence bubbles located in different areas. Each piece of evidence provides information that may help explain the flooding and can later be used as supporting evidence in the scientific argument.

4. When players collect an evidence bubble, DANI explains what the observation represents and provides contextual hints about how it may relate to the flooding. Players can use these explanations to distinguish relevant evidence from less useful information.

5. Players can combine multiple observations rather than relying on a single piece of evidence. Relevant information includes the bunker’s relationship to the groundwater-saturated zone, the presence of permeable material such as sand around or above the bunker, and the open or missing ceiling that provides a pathway for water to enter.

6. Players can review Dr. Toppo’s earlier instructional content and draw on knowledge from previous groundwater and soil puzzles, particularly concepts involving the water table, saturated zones, permeability, and groundwater movement.

7. DANI helps players review the core components of scientific argumentation—**claim, evidence, and reasoning**—before or during the argumentation task. This support can help players organize the collected observations into a coherent explanation.

8. The argumentation interface provides supporting background information related to the scientific concepts involved in the flooding problem. Players can consult this information when deciding which claim, evidence, and reasoning nodes belong in the argument.

9. Players can inspect the detailed content of each available argumentation node before selecting it. Because the strongest explanation requires **multiple pieces of evidence**, players should consider whether the selected evidence works together to support the same claim rather than choosing only one individually relevant observation.

10. After submitting the argument, Dr. Toppo provides feedback indicating whether the argument is correct. If the submission is incorrect, the feedback explains what aspect of the argument is incomplete or unsupported and provides guidance that can help the player revise the claim, evidence, or reasoning.

11. Players can combine the collected evidence, DANI’s explanations, groundwater knowledge, argumentation references, and Dr. Toppo’s feedback to progressively construct a complete explanation of why the bunker continues to flood even after the fountain is turned off.

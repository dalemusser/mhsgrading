# Information and Strategies Available to Players
1. Players can apply the topographic-map knowledge and map-reading skills learned during the previous progress point as a foundation for this navigation task.

2. Anderson provides narrative clues describing Dr. Toppo’s likely location. Players can review these clues and identify important geographic information, such as direction, nearby terrain features, elevation, or other recognizable landmarks.

3. DANI guides players in connecting Anderson’s narrative clues to information shown on the topographic map. This guidance may review what contour lines represent, how to interpret cardinal directions, and how to locate the dialogue or task information containing the relevant clues.

4. DANI also teaches players how to use the topographic map as a navigation tool. Players learn how to identify a likely destination, place a waypoint on the map, and use the waypoint to guide their movement through the game environment.

5. Players can compare the environmental features they encounter while traveling with the geographic features described in Anderson’s clues and represented on the map. This comparison can help them determine whether they are following an appropriate route.

6. If players travel in a direction or enter an area that is inconsistent with the provided clues, adaptive reminder dialogues may appear. These reminders explain why the current route is unlikely to lead to Dr. Toppo and direct players’ attention toward more relevant clues, geographic features, or directions.

7. Players can use the adaptive reminders to reconsider their interpretation of the clues, adjust the waypoint when necessary, and revise their route until they reach the correct location.

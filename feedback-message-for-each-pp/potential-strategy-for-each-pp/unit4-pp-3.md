# Information and Strategies Available to Players

1. After entering the dungeon, players first complete practice puzzles on Floors 1 and 2. These earlier floors allow them to become familiar with the mechanics of selecting soil materials and observing how those choices affect water movement through the pipe system and the movement of the key cube.

2. Players can use the practice floors to learn through observation and experimentation. By selecting different soil materials and watching the resulting water level and flow, they can develop a better understanding of which materials allow water to move more readily and which materials restrict or retain water.

3. Players can apply relevant knowledge from Dr. Toppo’s earlier tutorial and previous groundwater-related quests, particularly concepts involving soil permeability, water retention, and differences in how water moves through clay, sand, and gravel.

4. Players can use the in-game chat-history tool to review previous conversations and instructional information when they need to revisit groundwater or soil-property concepts.

5. Each time players operate the soil-selection machine, they can observe the effect of their selected material on the water system. They can use the resulting water level, water movement, and key-cube behavior as immediate feedback about whether their current choice is appropriate.

6. Players can compare the observed outcome with the goal of the puzzle. If the water level or flow is too low or too high for the key cube to move successfully through the pipe, they can infer that the current material is not producing the required condition.

7. Players can revise the soil selection based on the previous outcome and test a different material. Through this process of prediction, observation, and adjustment, they can progressively identify the soil condition that allows the key cube to reach the lock station.

8. Knowledge gained from the practice floors can be transferred to the more challenging third-floor puzzle, where players are expected to solve the system with greater independence and fewer unnecessary adjustments.

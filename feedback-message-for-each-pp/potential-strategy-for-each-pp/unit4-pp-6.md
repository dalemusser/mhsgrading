# Information and Strategies Available to Players

1. After Anderson is rescued from the bunker, Tara asks the player for help determining which soil type is most appropriate for several different plants. For each type of seed, Tara provides information about how much water the plant needs in order to grow successfully.

2. Players can apply knowledge gained from earlier Unit 4 puzzles about the properties of **sand, clay, and gravel**, particularly how these materials differ in their ability to retain water and allow water to drain or move through the soil.

3. Players can review Dr. Toppo’s earlier instructional content to reinforce relevant concepts about soil properties, groundwater movement, permeability, and water retention.

4. Players can also draw on their previous puzzle-solving experiences from the unit. Earlier activities provide opportunities to observe how different soil materials affect water movement and storage, which can help players predict which soil will provide suitable moisture conditions for each plant.

5. Players can use the in-game chat-history tool to review previous conversations with Tara and other NPCs. This allows them to revisit information about each plant’s water needs and any relevant explanations about soil characteristics.

6. To make each planting decision, players can compare the plant’s water requirement with the water-retention and drainage characteristics of the available soil types. Plants that require more available water may benefit from soil conditions that retain more moisture, while plants that require less water may be better suited to soils that allow greater drainage.

7. Players should evaluate the suitability of the soil based on the interaction between **plant water needs** and **soil properties**, rather than selecting a soil type based only on its name or previous use in another task.

8. There are three groups of seeds to evaluate. After the player selects a soil type for a particular seed group, Tara reviews the resulting plant-growth condition and provides feedback about whether the chosen soil is appropriate.

9. When a soil choice is not optimal, Tara explains why the selected soil does not provide the most suitable water condition for that plant. Players can use this feedback to refine their understanding of the relationship between soil water retention, drainage, and plant water needs.

10. Players can combine Tara’s descriptions of plant water requirements, previous Unit 4 experiences, Dr. Toppo’s instructional content, chat-history information, and Tara’s feedback to progressively identify the most appropriate soil type for all three seed groups.

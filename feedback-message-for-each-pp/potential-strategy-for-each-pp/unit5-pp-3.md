# Information and Strategies Available to Players

1. After completing the evaporation and condensation machine puzzles in the dungeon, players leave the dungeon and meet Aryn. Through conversations with Aryn, they learn more about the island and the water factory, including that the factory relies on water phase-change processes, such as condensation, to generate usable liquid water.

2. Players can draw on knowledge developed during the earlier Unit 5 activities, particularly the relationships among temperature, evaporation, condensation, liquid water, and water vapor. This prior knowledge provides the scientific foundation for explaining what happened to the water in the factory.

3. While exploring the factory, players collect multiple pieces of evidence related to why the liquid water has disappeared. Each observation provides information that can later be used to support the scientific argument.

4. When players collect evidence, DANI explains what the observation represents and provides hints about how it may relate to the disappearance of the water. Players can use these explanations to evaluate which observations are scientifically relevant.

5. Players can compare the collected evidence together rather than considering each observation independently. In particular, they can examine evidence related to **temperature and humidity** and consider how these observations support the possibility that liquid water changed into water vapor through evaporation.

6. Before entering the argumentation system, players receive conversations that review the key components of scientific argumentation—**claim, evidence, and reasoning**—and how those components should work together.

7. Within the argumentation system, players can carefully inspect the detailed content of each available claim, evidence, and reasoning node before making a selection. Because this activity contains more available choices than earlier argumentation tasks, comparing the meaning and function of the nodes can help players avoid selecting statements that are related to the topic but do not logically support the explanation.

8. The argumentation system provides background or reference information that players can consult to review relevant subject-matter knowledge about evaporation, condensation, temperature, humidity, and water phase changes.

9. Players should select a claim explaining that the missing liquid water underwent evaporation, choose reasoning that connects the environmental conditions to this phase change, and identify the multiple pieces of evidence that together provide the strongest support for the explanation.

10. After submitting the argument, Dr. Toppo provides feedback indicating whether the argument is correct. If the submission is incorrect, the feedback explains which part of the argument is unsupported, incomplete, or inconsistent with the evidence and provides guidance for revision.

11. Players can use Dr. Toppo’s feedback, DANI’s evidence explanations, the available background information, and their prior knowledge of evaporation and condensation to revise the argument until the claim, evidence, and reasoning form a coherent scientific explanation.
